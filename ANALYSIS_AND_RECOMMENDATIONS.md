generation) and wall-clock/compute cost.

**Date:** 2026-06-10
**Hardware on hand:** NVIDIA RTX A4500 (20 GB VRAM), 64 GB system RAM — GPU

## 0. Session outcome (2026-06-10 → 2026-06-18) — the plateau was broken: 0.116 → 0.1837 m (+58%)

The best-fitness plateau turned out to be a **body-encoder** problem, not a controller
problem. Fixes were implemented one at a time, each A/B-tested at pop 60 / 60 gens /
seed 42 (so runs share identical gen-1 bodies):

| step | change | best | what it did |
|---|---|---|---|
| baseline | legacy CPG controller | **0.116 m** | single-material blob; CPPN frozen at 0 hidden nodes |
| A1 | **open-loop material-phase** actuation (drop the CPG) | ~0.11 | better population mean + sample-efficiency; **not** the best-fitness fix (the §4.1 "CPG *is* the plateau" claim is **disproven**) |
| +A2 +speciation | presence/material split; NEAT speciation + fitness-sharing on AFPO | **0.152 m** | CPPN finally complexifies (N10→N14); two-material muscle bodies |
| +elitism | protect top-k by raw fitness in the AFPO truncation | **0.1726 m** | per-gen best now **monotonic** — the search stops discarding its best find |
| +rigid bone | stiff material kp 100→300 (real skeleton, not dead weight) | **0.1837 m** | bone adoption 0%→1%; modest lift (Cheney: bone "rarely decisive") |

**Defaults now:** open-loop actuation (`--use-cpg` for legacy CPG), speciation
(`--species-threshold`), elitism (`--elite`, default 2), auto-resume checkpointing
(`--no-resume` to disable), and Windows keep-awake (Modern Standby otherwise kills long
runs — they're run in the user's VS Code terminal).

**GPU/MJX evaluated and ruled out.** A WSL2 + jax[cuda12] feasibility spike on the A4500
showed MJX is ~140× *slower* than one CPU thread here and OOMs on real-size robots (the
batched dense constraint solver wants TiB); per-morphology recompile is ~123 s. The
"GPU is a realistic lever" framing above (line 8) is **wrong** for this problem — CPU is
the right tool. Benchmarks: `fair_mjx_bench.py`, `batch_scale_test.py`, `thorough_mjx_bench.py`.

**Still-open levers (new plateau ~0.18 m):** fitness shaping (directed distance, the
deferred ground-threshold bug, longer sim), more generations/seeds, larger population,
and a CPU-side flex/deformable physics reformulation for speed.

## 1. What this codebase is

A **MuJoCo re-implementation of Cheney et al. 2013, "Unshackling Evolution"** (GECCO):
evolve voxel-based soft robots whose 3-D body plans are generated by a **CPPN+NEAT**
genome, simulate them under gravity, and select for locomotion distance. The original
paper used Cornell's VoxCAD soft-body simulator; this project swaps in MuJoCo with a
spatial-tendon mass-spring approximation of soft voxels.

### Pipeline (end to end, as actually implemented)

```
CPPNGenome (NEAT network)                         src/evolution/cppn_genome.py
   │  evaluate_batch over (x,y,z,dist) for 5832 interior cells → 5 logits → argmax
   ▼
voxel grid (0=empty, 1=act0°, 2=act180°, 3=soft, 4=stiff)      to_voxel_grid()
   │  cap at MAX_VOXELS=300 by confidence; keep largest face-connected component
   ▼
MuJoCo XML  (one body + free joint + site per voxel,    mujoco_tendon_converter.py
             spatial tendons on face-adjacent pairs)
   │  active pair → position actuator; passive-passive → stiffness/damping spring
   ▼
MuJoCoTendonPhysics  (CG solver, dt=5e-4)               mujoco_tendon_physics.py
   │  per-step: ctrl = rest · (1 + amp · signal)
   ▼
fitness = horizontal_distance × ground_fraction         get_fitness()
   │  (early-terminate at 20% of window if displacement < 3 mm)
   ▼
Age-Fitness Pareto selection + inject 10%/gen           run_tendon_evolution.py
```

**Selection** is AFPO (Schmidt & Lipson, GECCO 2010): non-dominated sort on
(fitness ↑, age ↓), with fresh random robots injected each generation. Verified correct
against the paper — this is a sound choice and a legitimate "morphological innovation
protection" mechanism.

### Where the project stands empirically

The long `results/tendon_run/` run reached **best ≈ 0.065 m, mean ≈ 0.036 m** by
generation 227 and is clearly plateaued (gen 200–227 hover in the same band; see the
per-gen JSON files). At a ~5 cm body width that's roughly **1.3 body-lengths** of
travel. Cheney's champions reached **~3.5 body-lengths** over 1000 generations at
population 30. So the system works, but is leaving most of the achievable performance on
the table — and §4 explains the single biggest reason why.

## 2. Paper review (all five PDFs, read in full)

| Paper | What it gives this project | Fidelity of our implementation |
|---|---|---|
| **Cheney 2013 — Unshackling Evolution** | The core method. CPPN-NEAT body; **4 materials**: green = 20 % volumetric actuation, red = same but counter-phase, light-blue = soft passive, dark-blue = stiff passive. Pop 30, **1000 gens**, 35 runs. Fitness = COM displacement after **10 actuation cycles**. | Body encoding ✅. **Controller ❗** — see §4.1. Cheney has **no controller**: phase is fixed by material (green 0, red π) at one global frequency. We bolt on a random-init CPG that overrides those phases — a deviation that breaks the search. |
| **Corucci 2018 — Soft Locomotion Aquatic/Terrestrial** | Same VoxCAD/CPPN lineage; environment shapes morphology; runs of **5000–9000 gens**. Also uses material-phase actuation, no learned controller. | Confirms our generation budget (≤ 250 effective) is far too low (§5). We don't model fluid, which is fine for a terrestrial-only goal. |
| **Bhatia 2021 — Evolution Gym** | 32-task co-design benchmark; design-optimiser + **deep-RL or fixed controller**. Shows learned controllers can beat fixed oscillators *but* are far more expensive and unstable to co-optimise (BO surrogates "perform poorly… noisy RL evaluation"). | We use neither RL nor the EvoGym sim. A comparison point, not a constraint. Confirms that a *fixed, body-determined* controller (Cheney-style) is the cheap, robust baseline to nail first. |
| **Mouret & Clune 2015 — MAP-Elites** | Quality-Diversity: keep an archive of elites across a behaviour space; diverse "stepping stones" reach higher peaks than pure objective search. | Not used. Strong candidate even for a single champion (§4.4). The Nordmoen 2021 follow-up (§3) shows MAP-Elites finds *higher-performing* solutions, not just more diverse ones. |
| **Schmidt & Lipson 2010 — Age-Fitness Pareto** | The selection scheme we run: age as an explicit 2nd objective; inject randoms each gen; 3× less compute than ALPS to reach the global optimum. | Implemented ✅ and faithful. |

Two concrete fidelity gaps fall straight out of reading Cheney (both actionable, §4):

1. **No controller in the original.** "two types of voxels: those that expand and
   contract at a pre-specified frequency, and passive voxels… Green undergo periodic
   volumetric actuations of 20 %. Red behave similarly but with counter-phase." The
   "brain" is *just the material map*. Our CPG is an unfaithful addition (§4.1).
2. **Presence is a separate output from material.** Cheney: "One output specifies
   whether any material is present, while the maximum value of the 4 remaining outputs
   specifies the type… This separation has been shown to improve performance."
   We instead argmax over 5 logits (empty as class 0), which couples presence and
   material selection (§4.3).

## 3. Additional literature found (2021–2026)

The dominant modern thread is exactly our failure mode: **brain–body co-optimisation
suffers "fragile co-adaptation" → premature convergence.**

- **Mertan & Cheney 2024 — Investigating Premature Convergence in Co-optimization of
  Morphology and Control in Evolved Virtual Soft Robots** (EuroGP). Direct, recent study
  of this exact problem on voxel softbots. Finding: high-performing morphology regions
  *exist and are easily found when optimising morphology alone, but cannot be discovered
  when co-optimising body and control.* Strongly implies: get the controller out of the
  inner loop. <https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.09231>
- **Cheney, Bongard, SunSpiral, Lipson 2018 — Scalable Co-Optimization / "morphological
  innovation protection."** Resetting a body's controller throws away good bodies;
  protecting young morphologies fixes it. AFPO is one such mechanism (we have it).
  <https://arxiv.org/pdf/1706.06133>
- **Nordmoen et al. 2021 — MAP-Elites Enables Powerful Stepping Stones and Diversity for
  Modular Robotics** (Frontiers Robotics & AI). QD finds *higher-performing* solutions
  than objective-only search and regains performance fastest under environment change.
  <https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/frobt.2021.639173/full>
- **MuJoCo MJX / MuJoCo Playground / MuJoCo-Warp** — JAX/XLA and CUDA re-implementations
  of MuJoCo that batch thousands of simulations on one GPU; 1–2 orders of magnitude over
  multi-threaded CPU for the parallel-rollout regime an evolutionary inner loop needs.
  This is the single biggest compute lever given the A4500 (§5 Track B).
  <https://mujoco.readthedocs.io/en/stable/mjx.html> ·
  <https://github.com/google-deepmind/mujoco_warp>
- **Surrogate-assisted EAs** — learn a cheap body-features → fitness predictor to skip
  expensive sims. EvoGym's own results warn that surrogates over *RL-noisy* fitness fit
  poorly; our fitness is *deterministic*, so a surrogate is more viable here than there.
- **BodyGen 2025 / RoboMorph 2024** — transformer/LLM-driven morphology co-design.
  Interesting but a different paradigm; out of scope for a faithful Cheney reproduction.

## 4. Key findings in the code (highest-impact first)

### 4.1 ❗❗ The CPG controller is the plateau — and it's not even in the original paper

> **⚠️ CORRECTED 2026-06-15 (see §0):** the A/B test disproved the "is *the* plateau"
> claim. Both CPG and open-loop plateau in *best* fitness at ~0.11–0.12 m. The CPG is a
> real handicap on *population quality and sample-efficiency* (open-loop mean 0.088 vs
> 0.072) and on Cheney-fidelity — so A1 is kept as the default — but it is **not** the
> cause of the champion-fitness ceiling. Read the rest of this section as "why A1 still
> helps," not "why A1 breaks the plateau."

This is the headline finding, and reading the actual Cheney paper sharpens it
considerably versus the prior draft.

**What Cheney does:** there is no controller. A green voxel actuates at phase 0, a red
voxel at phase π, every voxel at one shared frequency. Phase is a pure function of the
material at a location, so it is *automatically inherited* whenever the body changes —
the body and the "brain" are the same object.

**What this code does instead:** every robot gets a `CPGController`
(`src/evolution/controllers.py`) with one oscillator per active tendon, holding random
per-tendon `frequencies`, `phases`, and a coupling matrix. Then:

- `crossover_controller()` and `_reconcile_controller()` (in
  `run_tendon_evolution.py` and `mujoco_tendon_evaluator.py`) build a **fresh random
  controller whenever the child's active-tendon count differs from the parents'.**
- Almost every CPPN mutation changes the body → changes the active-tendon count → **the
  brain is re-randomised nearly every generation.**

Consequence: a good body is evaluated with a random brain, scores poorly, and is
discarded; the body can never accumulate improvement because its controller keeps
resetting. This is textbook **fragile co-adaptation / premature convergence**
(Mertan & Cheney 2024; Cheney 2018) and is almost certainly the dominant reason mean
fitness flatlines around gen 200.

**The fix is a deletion, not an addition.** The open-loop sinusoidal path already exists
and is already correct — `apply_actuation()` in `mujoco_tendon_physics.py` has an `else`
branch that drives `ctrl = rest · (1 + amp · sin(ωt + base_phase))` using the
material-derived `base_phase`. It is **currently dead code**, because a controller is
always attached. Removing the CPG and using that path:

- deletes the reset-the-brain bottleneck entirely;
- makes the antiphase materials real again (§4.2);
- is the **most faithful possible** reproduction of Cheney 2013;
- removes the global-`np.random` reproducibility bug (§4.5) for free;
- deletes `controllers.py`, `make/mutate/crossover_controller`, and the whole
  reconciliation path.

If you later want *evolvable* gait (beyond fixed material phase), the faithful upgrade is
to **add one or two CPPN output heads for phase/frequency as a function of (x,y,z)** —
HyperNEAT-style, exactly the "co-evolve controller and morphology via the same CPPN"
direction Cheney names in his Future Work. Because it's keyed by position, it is still
inherited across body changes. That is the principled version of A1 if fixed phase isn't
expressive enough.

### 4.2 ❗ Materials 1 and 2 are behaviourally identical during evolution

`mujoco_tendon_converter.py` assigns `base_phase` 0 (mat1) vs π (mat2), but
`apply_actuation()` **ignores `base_phase` whenever a controller is attached** — which is
always. The CPG injects its own random phases, so mat1 vs mat2 carry **no behavioural
difference**, and the CPPN wastes one of five output classes distinguishing them. This is
the single most visible symptom of 4.1 and is fixed for free by removing the CPG.

### 4.3 The CPPN decode couples presence and material; Cheney separates them

`to_voxel_grid()` does `argmax` over 5 logits (class 0 = empty). Cheney uses **1 presence
output + 4 material outputs**, argmax over the 4 only when present, and reports this
separation "has been shown to improve performance" (Verbancsics & Stanley 2011). Concrete
faithful change: 6 outputs = `[presence, m1, m2, m3, m4]`; voxel exists iff
`presence > 0`, material = argmax of the four. Cheap; modestly improves evolvability.

### 4.4 Search would converge higher with a Quality-Diversity layer (even for one champion)

Nordmoen 2021 shows MAP-Elites finds *higher-performing* champions than objective-only
search via stepping stones. A light QD archive with behaviour descriptors (e.g.
body-height × actuated-fraction, or gait frequency × body length) complements AFPO.
Medium effort, high upside, and it uses the MAP-Elites paper already in the folder.

### 4.5 Lower-impact issues

- **Silent failures:** `_eval_worker` catches *all* exceptions and returns `0.0` with no
  logging — a crashing robot is indistinguishable from a non-mover. Add a counter/log so
  systematic breakage isn't hidden behind "it just scored zero."
- **Non-reproducible controllers:** `CPGController` uses global `np.random`, not the
  seeded `rng`, so `--seed` doesn't fully reproduce runs. (Disappears with 4.1.)
- **Per-generation pool churn:** `evaluate_batch` does `with Pool(self.n_workers)` on
  every call, so the process pool is created and torn down each generation. On Windows
  spawn this re-imports MuJoCo/numpy in every worker every generation. Reuse one
  persistent pool for the whole run.
- **Fitness direction is fine as-is:** `sqrt(dx²+dy²)` net displacement matches Cheney's
  "difference in COM between initialisation and end" (also undirected). The prior draft's
  "make it directed" suggestion is *optional*, not a fidelity fix — only adopt it if you
  specifically want straight-line crawlers over spinners.
- **Generation budget:** Cheney 1000, Corucci 5000–9000; our effective budget is ≤ 250.
  The `--seed-from` warm-start you built is the right tool to chain toward those counts.
- **Deferred `ground_threshold` bug** (`initial_pos[2]*3.0` inflated by mid-bounce
  sampling) — left as-is per your standing instruction.

### 4.6 Architectural note: free-body-per-voxel + tendons is the root compute cost

Each voxel is a **free rigid body (6 DOF)** held together only by spatial-tendon springs.
A 300-voxel robot is ~1800 DOF plus a ground-contact constraint for every grounded voxel
plus a position-actuator constraint per active tendon. That ill-conditioned constraint
system is why the CG solver scales **superlinearly** (your measured 2.4× tendons → ~300×
slower) and why stability needed the kv/density tuning. MuJoCo 3 ships **native
deformable bodies (`flexcomp`/flex)** that represent a soft voxel lattice as one
conditioned deformable object — typically far faster and more stable than N free bodies +
tendons, and it maps better onto MJX. This is the highest-ceiling change for both speed
and stability, and is called out as option B5 below.

## 5. Recommendations — prioritised

### Track A — Evolutionary effectiveness (a better champion)

| # | Change | Effort | Expected effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A1** | **Delete the CPG; use the existing open-loop material-phase actuation** (§4.1, §4.2). Restores Cheney fidelity and removes the reset-the-brain bottleneck. | **S** | **Largest.** This is the single highest-leverage change in the repo. |
| A2 | **Separate presence/material CPPN outputs** (§4.3). | S | Small–medium; faithful, improves evolvability. |
| A3 | **Chain runs with `--seed-from`** toward 500–1000 effective gens (§4.5). | — | Already built; the plateau is partly just too few gens. |
| A4 | **MAP-Elites archive** over current operators (§4.4). | M–L | Medium–large; higher peaks via stepping stones. |
| A5 | *(Optional, only if fixed phase underperforms)* **CPPN phase/frequency output heads** — evolvable gait that's still position-keyed and inheritance-safe (§4.1). | M | Medium; the principled way to add a "brain" without re-introducing fragility. |

### Track B — Compute / wall-clock (you have a 20 GB A4500 — use it)

| # | Change | Effort | Expected effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| **B1** | **MJX batched evaluation on the GPU.** `mjx_prototype.py` already shows the batched-vmap win on a 300-DOF chain. Port the tendon model to MJX and evaluate the whole population in one `vmap`. Watch contact-Jacobian memory (the prototype's noted OOM risk); mitigate by limiting ground-contact geoms to bottom-layer voxels or capping `nconmax`. | M–L | **Largest compute win** — 1–2 orders of magnitude for the parallel-rollout regime. |
| B2 | **Persistent worker pool** (§4.5) — one `Pool` for the whole run, not per generation. | S | Medium on the CPU path; removes per-gen spawn/import overhead. |
| B3 | **Two-stage evaluation** — short cheap sim for all, full sim only for top-k. Most of any population are non-movers (the early-terminate already hints at this). | M | Large when the population is non-mover-dominated. |
| B4 | **Deterministic-fitness surrogate** — body features → predicted fitness to pre-screen losers. More viable here than in EvoGym because our fitness is noise-free. | L | Large but complex; do after B1–B3. |
| B5 | **Native MuJoCo deformable bodies (`flexcomp`)** instead of free-body-per-voxel + tendons (§4.6). | L | Largest ceiling for speed *and* stability; a real redesign — prototype alongside, don't rip-and-replace. |

### Suggested order

1. **A1** — delete the CPG (biggest effect, smallest effort; do this first and re-run the
   227-gen baseline to measure the lift).
2. **B2** — persistent pool (trivial, pure speed) and **A2** (cheap fidelity).
3. **A3** — chain warm-started runs toward Cheney-scale generation counts.
4. **B1** — MJX port for the order-of-magnitude throughput jump on the A4500.
5. Then evaluate **A4 (MAP-Elites)** and **B3/B4/B5** based on where the bottleneck sits
   after the above.
